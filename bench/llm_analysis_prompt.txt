## Background

This is a word-level forced-alignment system for short (10-30s) clips of natural,
conversational English speech (interview-style, contains disfluencies, false starts,
fillers like "um"/"uh", truncated words like "s-", repeated words, etc.). The pipeline:

1. A wav2vec2 CTC model does forced alignment against a known word sequence, producing
   rough per-word start/end timestamps ("RAW CTC" in the data) at ~20ms frame resolution.
   These are frequently off by tens of milliseconds, especially at word boundaries next
   to silence, fillers, or disfluencies.
2. A large hand-written heuristic post-processing layer ("snap_boundaries_hybrid" in
   forced_aligner.py, not included here) refines these raw CTC boundaries using acoustic
   signals -- but its current output is NOT included in this data export. You are working
   from RAW CTC + the acoustic signal traces + gold labels, to find patterns the heuristic
   layer should implement, independent of its current (imperfect) logic.
3. "GOLD TOKENS" are hand-labeled ground-truth boundaries from a human annotator. The goal
   is to get automated boundaries as close to these as practically possible.

## IMPORTANT: gold labels are not perfect

The human annotator's labels have their own imprecision -- they were placed by ear/eye in
a labeling tool, not derived from a formal acoustic model. In earlier analysis, we found:
- For some pairs, an independent multi-signal "consensus" boundary point disagreed with
  BOTH the algorithm's prediction AND gold, suggesting gold itself was not exactly at the
  acoustically "true" transition.
- For some pairs (e.g. two adjacent instances of the same fricative merging, like "was
  still", or "little room" where a liquid consonant flows into another liquid), there is
  NO acoustically recoverable boundary at all -- the audio is genuinely continuous/uniform
  across the whole span, and gold's specific split point appears arbitrary.
- Do NOT try to build a rule that exactly reproduces every single gold boundary. Look for
  the acoustically well-motivated general signal that explains the MAJORITY of cases in a
  given phonetic category, and flag/set aside cases that look genuinely ambiguous (long
  spans with completely flat, unchanging signals) rather than force-fitting them.

## What "good" looks like

We are NOT looking for a lookup table or per-clip/per-word special case. We want signal
processing RULES of the form: "when word A ends in phonetic-class X and word B starts with
phonetic-class Y, look for signal-condition Z in the region around the raw CTC boundary,
and place the boundary there." Rules should be:
- Expressed in terms of the 6 signals provided (see below), thresholds/relative comparisons
  on them, and simple phonetic categories inferable from the word text (e.g. "ends in a
  stop consonant", "starts with a sibilant") -- NOT specific words or specific clips.
- Robust to the fact that ZCR and voicing (autocorrelation-based periodicity) become noisy
  and spuriously elevated at near-total silence / near-zero RMS. Any rule using those two
  signals should gate them behind a minimum-RMS floor (e.g. only trust ZCR/voicing once RMS
  is some fraction of a local or clip-level energy threshold) -- this exact failure mode has
  burned us multiple times already.
- Validated by you checking the rule against MULTIPLE clips/word-pairs in the data, not
  just the one example that inspired it, since the existing system has repeatedly found
  fixes that work on one diagnosed case but regress many others sharing the same code path.

## Data format

The attached file has one block per clip in this batch, each containing:

1. **GOLD TOKENS**: `idx  text  start_s  end_s` -- the ground-truth word boundaries you are
   trying to explain/predict, in clip-local seconds.

2. **RAW CTC**: `idx  ctc_word_text  word_start_s  word_end_s  word_score  pipe_start_s  pipe_end_s`
   -- the wav2vec2 model's own raw per-word timestamps (idx aligned to GOLD TOKENS' idx),
   BEFORE any heuristic refinement. `ctc_word_text` is the cleaned/uppercased text the CTC
   decoder was forced to align against (may differ cosmetically from gold text, e.g. spelled
   numbers, letter names for single letters like "t"->"TEE"). `pipe_start`/`pipe_end` mark
   the CTC "blank"/silence span between this word and the next one. `word_score` is the
   CTC's own per-word confidence (lower = more uncertain).

3. **WHISPER (Groq) OUTPUT**: `word  start_s  end_s` -- an independent ASR pass (OpenAI
   Whisper via Groq) over the same audio, with its OWN word segmentation (may split/merge
   words differently than gold or CTC, may hallucinate/repeat words, has looser timestamp
   precision). NOT index-aligned to gold/CTC -- match by approximate time/text if useful.
   Provided as a second, independent opinion on word timing/content, for cases where CTC
   and gold disagree and a third signal might help adjudicate.

4. **SIGNAL TRACE**: `time_s  rms  zcr  voicing  spectral_flux  hf_ratio  spectral_flatness`
   at 5ms resolution across the whole clip. Definitions:
   - `rms`: short-time energy (2ms window). Silence/voiceless-stop-closures are near 0;
     voiced speech and fricatives are elevated, though QUIET fricatives can have RMS not
     much above silence -- this is precisely why RMS alone fails for fricative boundaries.
   - `zcr`: zero-crossing rate (5ms window). High for fricatives/sibilants and unvoiced
     noise, low for voiced speech/silence -- BUT becomes noisy/spuriously high at near-zero
     RMS (silence), so must be gated by an RMS floor to be trustworthy.
   - `voicing`: normalized autocorrelation peak in the human pitch range (70-400Hz), 20ms
     frames. High (0.5-0.9) for genuinely voiced speech, low for silence/voiceless sounds --
     but ALSO becomes spuriously elevated at near-zero RMS (same artifact as zcr). Must be
     RMS-gated too.
   - `spectral_flux`: frame-to-frame spectral-shape change (L2 norm of positive-diffs of the
     normalized magnitude spectrum). Large values indicate a sudden timbral/onset change,
     regardless of absolute energy level.
   - `hf_ratio`: fraction of spectral energy above 4kHz. Near 0 for voiced speech/silence,
     elevated (often 0.3-0.99) for frication/aspiration (sibilants /s,z,sh,ch/, non-sibilant
     fricatives /f,v,th/, and stop-release aspiration) -- this has been our single most
     useful signal for finding frication boundaries that RMS/ZCR miss, but it ALSO becomes
     erratic at near-zero RMS, same gating caveat applies.
   - `spectral_flatness`: geometric-mean/arithmetic-mean of the spectrum (noise-like vs.
     tonal). Low (~0.02-0.1) for clean voiced/tonal content, higher (0.2-0.4+) for
     noisy/breathy/fricative content. Empirically useful for voiced-fricative-like sounds
     (e.g. the voiced "th" in "the/that/this/then/...") where periodicity (voicing) stays
     high WHILE flatness also rises -- that simultaneous signature is a clean marker for
     voiced-frication onsets, distinct from a plain vowel-to-vowel transition.

   `trail_thresh` and `speech_thresh` (printed once per clip, near the top) are the current
   system's own clip-calibrated RMS thresholds (roughly: below trail_thresh = "likely
   silence", above speech_thresh = "confidently speech"), given as reference scale, not as
   the only valid thresholds -- feel free to propose your own relative/absolute thresholds
   if better ones are apparent from the data.
